Background
Low back pain is a leading cause of disability. Chronic low back pain (CLBP) is a persistent pain in the lumbar region lasting for longer than 3-6 months. Vertebrogenic CLBP is thought to be a distinct type of back pain, originating from degeneration of the vertebral body or vertebral body endplates, resulting in inflammation. The inflammatory response is perceived by the basivertebral nerve (BVN), a sensory nerve that enters the posterior vertebral body and branches out to the superior and inferior endplates. Pain signals are then transmitted to the central nervous system, causing vertebrogenic pain (Fischgrund et al., 2019). Diagnosis of CLBP and its underlying cause is initiated with a comprehensive history and physical examination. Testing may include bloodwork, standard radiographs, bone scans, computerized axial tomography scans, MRI, electromyography, or a diagnostic injection (NASS, 2022). CLBP can be treated with physical therapy (e.g., ice, heat, massage, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation [TENS], ultrasound), analgesia, anti-inflammatory medications, muscle-relaxing agents, coping skills, minimally invasive injections or procedures (e.g., nerve ablation), surgery, or complementary medicine (e.g., acupuncture, dry needling, magnets, etc.) (NINDS, 2022). Intraosseous BVN ablation using a radiofrequency probe and generator has been suggested as a minimally invasive
treatment for CLBP in a subset of patients with specific clinical indications.

Background
Fischgrund et. al (2018) conducted a randomized double blind sham controlled multi-center study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of radiofrequency (RF) ablation of the basivertebral nerve (BVN) for the treatment of chronic low back pain (CLBP) in a Food and Drug Administration approved Investigational Device Exemption trial. A total of 225 patients diagnosed with CLBP were randomized to either a sham (78 patients) or treatment (147 patients) intervention. The mean age within the study was 47 years (range 25-69) and the mean baseline ODI was 42. All patients had Type I or Type II Modic changes of the treated vertebral bodies. Patients were evaluated preoperatively, and at 2 weeks, 6 weeks and 3, 6 and 12 months postoperatively. The primary endpoint was the comparative change in ODI from baseline to 3 months. At 3 months, the average ODI in the treatment arm decreased 20.5 points, as compared to a 15.2 point decrease in the sham arm (p = 0.019, per-protocol population). A responder analysis based on ODI decr ease ≥ 10 points showed that 75.6% of patients in the treatment arm as compared to 55.3% in the sham control arm exhibited a clinically meaningful improvement at 3 months. Conclusion: Patients treated with RF ablation of the BVN for CLBP exhibited significantly greater improvement in ODI at 3 months and a higher responder rate than sham treated controls. Comparisons between the post ablation and baseline values were made using an analysis of covariance with alpha 0.05. Results: Of the 117 US treated patients 100 (85%) were available for review with a mean follow-up of 6.4 years (5.4-7.8 years). Mean Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) score improved from 42.81 to 16.86 at 5year follow-up, a reduction of 25.95 points (p < 0.001). Mean reduction in Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) pain score was 4.38 points (baseline of 6.74, p < 0.001). In total, 66% of patients reported a > 50% reduction in pain, 47% reported a > 75% reduction in pain, and 34% of patients reported complete pain r esolution. Composite responder rate using thresholds of ≥ 15 - point ODI and ≥ 2 -point VAS for function and pain at 5 years was 75%. Conclusion: CLBP patients treated with BVN ablation exhibit sustained clinical improvements in function and pain with high responder rates at a mean of 6.4 years following treatment.
