Table 2 examines the prevalence of employer-sponsored benefits in the sample. Paid vacation or paid sick leave is the most commonly offered benefit among respondents, with just under three-quarters of the sample indicating that this is a benefit their employer provides. Approximately two-thirds of the sample has access to health insurance through their employer, 61% report access to a defined contribution plan, and 51% report access to employer-sponsored life insurance. Less than half the observations have access to disability insurance, while a third indicate that they have a benefit of working-from-home. Relatively few respondents indicate access to student loan repayment, emergency savings accounts, and childcare benefits.

As might be expected, there are positive correlations across access to employersponsored benefits; in other words, if an employer offers certain benefits, it is more likely to offer certain others. Table 3 presents the correlation matrix for the six most commonly provided benefits in the sample. Findings indicate moderately strong correlations across most of these benefits. Employers who provide paid sick leave or paid vacation are, for example, more likely to also provide health insurance. Work-from-home (WFH) benefits are less strongly correlated with other benefits under study. Results show weaker, though still positive, correlations between the less commonly offered benefits - student loan repayment, emergency savings accounts, childcare and others (not shown).
