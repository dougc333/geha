## VI. Results: Employer-Sponsored Benefits and Employee Turnover

The analysis finds substantial, and persistent, racial and ethnic gaps in access to employersponsored benefits, and that employer-sponsored benefits are systematically linked to higher employee financial security.  This section  examines how employer benefits are related to employee turnover.  To address this question, the paper leverages questions in the 2022 and 2023 surveys eliciting whether respondents changed employers over the last 12 months.  Leveraging these data, it examines how benefit access in 2021 and 2022 influences whether an individual changes employers in the next year.  Approximately 9 percent of individuals changed employers in 2022 and 2023.

To examine associations between employer benefits and employee turnover, the paper estimates equation (1) above.  Table 7 presents results.  Findings show that, outside of employersponsored emergency savings accounts, all the benefits that are examined are at least directionally associated with lower employee turnover.  Two of the estimated associations are statistically significant - paid vacation or sick leave and an ability to work from home are associated with a 2.3 - 2.4 percentage point reduction in the likelihood of leaving one's employer in the next year, corresponding to an approximate 25% reduction in the sample mean turnover.
