Table 1 presents summary statistics for the sample.  To focus on access to, and the impacts of, employer-sponsored benefits, the paper restrict attention to individuals who were employed at the time of the survey. 3 In total, we have over 11,000 person-year observations. Average age in the sample is 46 years old and 58% of the sample is female.  A little less than two-thirds of observations are non-Hispanic white adults, 57% are married, and 11% self-report to be in poor health.  Consistent with restricting the sample to employed individuals, the sample is a little higher educated and higher income than the population writ large; 52% of observations have a bachelor's degree or more education and 40% have a household income of $100,000 or more, though there is variation along both dimensions.  Equal proportions of the sample, 34%, are financially stressed and have high financial satisfaction, while 52% report actively saving for emergencies.
