Figure 6 explores gaps in access to work-from-home benefits across race/ethnicity and sex over time. In 2021, we observe a relatively modest, and marginally significant, 3 percentage-point gap between non-Hispanic white adults and the other groups, which grows to 5 – 6 percentage points in 2022 and 2023 (both statistically significant). In contrast, while men were 4 percentage points more likely to report an ability to work-from-home than women in 2021, this difference reduced to 1 – 2 percentage points in 2022 and 2023 and was no longer statistically significant.
