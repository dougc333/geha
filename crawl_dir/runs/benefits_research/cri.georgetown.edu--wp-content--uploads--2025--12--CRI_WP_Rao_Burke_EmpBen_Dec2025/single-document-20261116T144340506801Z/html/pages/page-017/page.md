Table 5 examines associations between employer-sponsored benefits and financial stress. Paid vacation or sick leave, access to a defined contribution plan, and access to health, life, and disability insurance are all statistically significantly associated with a lower likelihood of being financially stressed.  For example, those working for an employer who provides paid leave are 3.7 percentage points less likely to report 'high' or 'moderate' amounts of stress due to their financial situation (an 11% reduction relative to the sample mean).  There are also large, estimated effects for access to an emergency savings account and childcare benefits, though neither estimate is statistically significant given a lack of statistical precision due to these benefits being relatively scarce in the sample.  Similar to the analysis for financial satisfaction, the findings show that all the employer-sponsored benefits studied are at least directionally positively related to financial security - in this case negatively related to financial stress.

Table 6 explores how employer-sponsored benefits relate to emergency savings behavior. The findings show a clear relationship - employer-sponsored benefits are associated with a higher likelihood of saving for emergencies.  Paid vacation or sick leave, access to a defined contribution plan, an ability to work from home, student loan repayment benefits, childcare benefits and access to health, life, and disability insurance are all significantly associated with saving for emergencies.  For example, an ability to work from home is associated with a 5.6 percentage point increase in the likelihood of actively saving for emergencies (an 11 percent increase relative to the sample mean).  Thus, our results are consistent with the possibility that employer-sponsored benefits help facilitate savings behavior.  Working from home, for example, may reduce employees' transportation costs, increasing the likelihood they devote those resources towards savings.  While encouraging for employees who work at firms that offer such benefits, it is also somewhat concerning for those who do not.
