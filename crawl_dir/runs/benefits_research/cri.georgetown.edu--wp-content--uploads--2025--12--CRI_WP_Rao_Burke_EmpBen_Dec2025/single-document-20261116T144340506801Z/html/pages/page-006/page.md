The first asks respondents, ' Overall, how satisfied are you with your current financial situation?' on a 1 - 5 scale from 'extremely satisfied' to 'not at all satisfied . ' The second asks 'how much stress, if any, do your finances cause you?' with response options of 'high stress,' 'moderate stress,' 'some stress,' or 'no stress.' Responses are coded as high financial satisfaction if respondents report being either 'extremely satisfied' or 'very satisfied' with their financial situation. Responses are coded as financially stressed if respondents report that their financial situation is causing them either 'moderate' or 'high' stress. The paper also measures whether respondents are actively saving for emergencies by asking, ' A re you currently setting aside money for an emergency?' In 2023, the question was changed to elicit whether respondents are regularly setting aside money, setting aside money when they can but not regularly, or not setting aside money.  Responses are coded as saving for emergencies if respondents report currently setting aside money in 2021 or 2022 or regularly doing so in 2023. 2 Importantly for the research questions examined in this paper, these surveys also ask respondents whether they have access to a litany of employer-sponsored benefits programs, whether or not they personally use them.  In particular, the surveys elicit access to paid vacation or sick leave, health insurance, life insurance, disability insurance, defined contribution retirement plans, student loan repayment, access to an emergency savings account or contributions toward emergency savings, childcare, and an ability to work-from-home.  There were a few changes to the nature of the questions over time.  In the first two waves of the data, paid vacation and sick leave were asked about separately, yet elicited jointly in the 2023 wave. Accordingly, an indicator was created for whether a respondent had access to either paid vacation or sick leave in the 2021 and 2022 waves to preserve comparability.  Additionally, the 2021 and 2022 survey waves ask if respondents' employers provided 'childcare' benefits, while the 2023 survey asks whether employers prov ide 'subsidies to pay for childcare.' The paper assesses these questions as substantively similar and combine across waves.  Access to (or

contributions towards) an emergency savings account was not asked in the 2021 wave.
