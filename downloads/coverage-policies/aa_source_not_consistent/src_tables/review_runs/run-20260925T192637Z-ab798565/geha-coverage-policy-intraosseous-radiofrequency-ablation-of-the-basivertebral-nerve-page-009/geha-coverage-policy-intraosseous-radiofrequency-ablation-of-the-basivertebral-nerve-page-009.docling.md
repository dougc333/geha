Fischgrund et. al (2018) conducted a randomized double blind sham controlled multi-center study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of radiofrequency (RF) ablation of the basivertebral nerve (BVN) for the treatment of chronic low back pain (CLBP) in a Food and Drug Administration approved Investigational Device Exemption trial. A total of 225 patients diagnosed with CLBP were randomized to either a sham (78 patients) or treatment (147 patients) intervention. The mean age within the study was 47 years (range 25-69) and the mean baseline ODI was 42. All patients had Type I or Type II Modic changes of the treated vertebral bodies. Patients were evaluated preoperatively, and at 2 weeks, 6 weeks and 3, 6 and 12 months postoperatively. The primary endpoint was the comparative change in ODI from baseline to 3 months. At 3 months, the average ODI in the treatment arm decreased 20.5 points, as compared to a 15.2 point decrease in the sham arm (p = 0.019, per-protocol population). A responder analysis based on ODI decr ease ≥ 10 points showed that 75.6% of patients in the treatment arm as compared to 55.3% in the sham control arm exhibited a clinically meaningful improvement at 3 months. Conclusion: Patients treated with RF ablation of the BVN for CLBP exhibited significantly greater improvement in ODI at 3 months and a higher responder rate than sham treated controls. BVN ablation represents a potential minimally invasive treatment for the relief of chronic low back pain. In a follow up study by Fischgurd et. al (2020), the long-term outcomes following intraosseous basivertebral nerve ablation for the treatment of chronic low back pain were assessed: 5-year treatment arm results from a prospective randomized double-blind sham-controlled multi-center study. In total, 117 US patients were treated successfully with BVN ablation. Patient-reported outcomes of post ablation treatments, and patient satisfaction were collected at a minimum of 5-years following BVN ablation. Comparisons between the post ablation and baseline values were made using an analysis of covariance with alpha 0.05. Results: Of the 117 US treated patients 100 (85%) were available for review with a mean follow-up of 6.4 years (5.4-7.8 years). Mean Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) score improved from 42.81 to 16.86 at 5year follow-up, a reduction of 25.95 points (p &lt; 0.001). Mean reduction in Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) pain score was 4.38 points (baseline of 6.74, p &lt; 0.001). In total, 66% of patients reported a &gt; 50% reduction in pain, 47% reported a &gt; 75% reduction in pain, and 34% of patients reported complete pain r esolution. Composite responder rate using thresholds of ≥ 15 - point ODI and ≥ 2 -point VAS for function and pain at 5 years was 75%. Conclusion: CLBP patients treated with BVN ablation exhibit sustained clinical improvements in function and pain with high responder rates at a mean of 6.4 years following treatment. BVN ablation is a durable, minimally invasive treatment for vertebrogenic CLBP.
